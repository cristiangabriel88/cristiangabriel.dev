Where it comes from

Three sources,
no catalogue.

Nothing is bought new except the socket, the flex and the oil. Everything else is found, and what it was stays visible.

Found wood

Gathered by hand along riverbanks and woodlot edges, in the week after storms. Only wood that is already dead and loose. Never cut, never taken off anything still living.

  • Weathered hardwood branch · walnut, oak, plum
  • Bases are slices off a fallen trunk, bark left on
  • Air-dried 6–9 months, no kiln
  • Cracks left open, not resin-poured

Repurposed metal

Pulled from scrapyards and farm sheds. Degreased, wire-brushed by hand, then clear-lacquered with the pitting left exactly as found. That surface took forty years to make.

  • Dacia 1300 pistons, bezels, rocker arms
  • Tractor gearbox spindles · belt pulleys
  • Brass unions, copper brake line
  • Never sandblasted, never chromed

Oddities

The drawer everything else lives in. Some of it waits two years for the right piece of wood to turn up, and some of it never gets used at all.

  • Porcelain fuse holders, 1960s substation
  • Bakelite toggle switches
  • Brass sextant vernier plates
  • Telegraph insulators, lab glass

How a lamp happens

Months of waiting,
two weeks of work.

The order matters. Skip the drying and the wood splits around the socket a year later, in someone's living room.

01

Finding

Walked after storms, along water and woodlot edges. Only what is already dead and loose. Most of what I pick up goes back.

02

Curing

Washed, bark taken back to the hard wood underneath, then stacked in an open shed with air on all four sides. No kiln: kiln-dried branch wood checks along the grain.

03

The base

A slice off a fallen trunk, bark left on the edge. Flattened by hand until it sits on a table without a single rock.

04

Fitting

The branch decides where the socket goes, not the drawing. Blind mortise into the base, brass E27 with a key switch, nothing structural held by glue.

05

Rope and wiring

Hemp rope spiralled from socket to base with the flex inside it. Strain relief at both ends, then continuity- and insulation-tested before it leaves.

06

Finish

Three coats of wood oil, 24 hours between, cut back with sandpaper. No stain, ever. That honey colour is the wood and the oil, nothing else.

The workshop

One bench,
one pair of hands.

The bench is outside, under a walnut. The first time a finished lamp is switched on, it is switched on in the yard.

I don't make series. Two lamps off the same tree will still be two different lamps, and if you ask me for a matching pair I'll tell you honestly which one to take. What you get is a signed piece, a written note of where every part of it came from, and someone who will rewire it for you in ten years.
